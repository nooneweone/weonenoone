Part 01 · Project Identity & Mission

This author-developed framework organizes eighteen philosophical, scientific, and conceptual themes as eighteen original music-and-video works. Each work is intended to be more than background music: the selected subject becomes the organizing idea of the music, while instruments, effects, environmental recordings, voices, material sounds, and silence act as expressive languages for that subject.

The creative aim is for a listener to sense the identity and world of a subject through the music, even without seeing its title or accompanying image. The eighteen works belong to one project but are designed to retain distinct sonic identities.

THE SUBJECT ITSELF BECOMES THE MUSIC.
Discover a wide field of possible sounds; select only what serves the subject; transform sound into music; create eighteen distinct sonic worlds.

This page presents the creative production framework documented in the author's MASTER PROMPT VERSION 9.0. It is a companion methodology for the ONE SYMBOL. INFINITE WORLDS. project, not an official OntoMesh technical specification or a validated production standard.

01

User Inputs Only Three Items

For each production, the user supplies only the following three items:

  1. A target collection number from 01 to 18.
  2. Image 1: a representative image showing the overall identity of the selected work.
  3. Image 2: a concept image showing the six areas within that collection.

The official collection data and six named areas are taken from this framework. No additional user-supplied genre, instrument list, effect list, technical format, or filename is required by the documented input protocol.

Image 1 · Overall Identity

Read the overall visual world, atmosphere, identity, symbolic elements, and the feeling the music should communicate.

Image 2 · Six-Area Structure

Read the six areas, their visual differences, relationships, and symbols that may inform music and sound design. When text in an image is unclear, the official collection data takes precedence.

03

Universal Sound Universe 13 Exploration Categories

Before producing a work, the framework calls for a broad exploration of sound. It does not require every category to appear in every composition. The objective is to discover possible sounds, select those most relevant to the subject, and integrate them musically.

01

Natural Sounds

Water drops, streams, rivers, waterfalls, waves, rain, thunder, wind, storms, leaves, moving trees, forest ambience, grass and flowers, sand, soil, stones, rocks, ground vibration, cracking ice, glaciers, fire, combustion, blizzards, and natural airflow.

02

Animal & Biological Sounds

Bird calls and wingbeats, bees and insects, frogs, cicadas, crickets, dogs and cats, wildlife, whales and marine organisms, animal movement, biological vocalizations, and ecosystem ambience.

03

Human Voice & Body Sounds

Laughter, smiling laughter, crying, sobbing, screams, cheers, whispers, conversation, crowd murmur, monologue, singing, humming, breathing, sighs, heartbeat, footsteps, clapping, finger snaps, finger taps, bodily movement, and clothing friction. Human voice should have a clear musical role or meaning.

04

Object & Material Sounds

Impacts between metal, glass, wood, and stone; plastic impacts; crumpling paper; fabric friction; breaking glass; object breakage; tearing paper; scraping metal; tapping wood; hard-surface impacts; soft contact; and material resonance.

05

Motion & Physical Sounds

Falling, dropping, bouncing, rolling, sliding, collision, impact, rupture, explosion, friction, rotation, shaking, vibration, pressure, contraction, expansion, acceleration, deceleration, splashing liquid, moving air, and colliding waves.

06

Mechanical & Industrial Sounds

Engines, motors, gears, machine operation and shutdown, pumps, factories, industrial equipment, conveyors, repetitive mechanical sounds, buttons, switches, doors, locks, vibrating metal structures, and mechanical impacts.

07

Time & Clock Sounds

Clock seconds and minute hands, chimes, alarms, timers, springs, gears, recurring time signals, pendulums, regular pulses, slowing ticks, accelerating clock rhythms, repetition, stopping, and abstract sounds of temporal flow.

08

Electronic & Digital Sounds

Beeps, electronic signals, digital data, glitches, static, electrical discharges, radio-like signals, pulses, electronic vibration, radar-like sounds, data transmission, digital interfaces, abstract synthesis, modulated machine sounds, and artificial rhythms.

09

Spatial & Architectural Sounds

Caves, tunnels, underground spaces, corridors, empty rooms, large halls, cathedrals, factory interiors, city streets, enclosed spaces, underwater spaces, open exteriors, small interiors, distant and close sounds, reflections, reverberation, and spatial movement.

10

Auditory & Perceptual Effects

A muffled impression, sudden distance, apparent sound blocking, underwater perspective, pressure, reduced or expanded space, distortion, auditory ringing, wavering sound, a confined-space impression, sudden disappearance, ambiguous sound presence, and loss of directional impression. These are to be created safely through audio processing rather than harmful physical stimulation.

11

Temporal & Transformation Effects

Reverse playback, time-stretching, time compression, gradual deceleration and acceleration, pitch rises and falls, repetition, fragmentation, rearrangement, sound emergence and disappearance, sudden stops, delayed responses, and transformation or rebirth of sound.

12

Silence & Micro-Sounds

Complete silence, brief pauses, extended silence, subtle breath, small friction, low vibration, tiny clicks, almost inaudible movement, the final moment of a long reverberation, and the space remaining after sound disappears.

13

Musical & Instrumental Sounds

Strings, woodwinds, brass, percussion, piano, organ, guitar, synthesizers, folk instruments, traditional percussion, metallic instruments, unconventional sound objects, prepared instruments, extended techniques, and processed instrumental sounds.

The thirteen categories are a starting taxonomy. New sound types may be added when they serve a selected collection. The amount of sound is not the goal; relevance and expressive function are the selection criteria.

04

Intelligent Sound Discovery & Selection

Sound discovery begins with an analysis of the selected subject. The production process considers:

  1. Which sounds can represent the subject?
  2. Which sounds have a direct relationship to the subject and its six areas?
  3. Which sounds may strengthen emotion and immersion?
  4. Which sounds can be transformed into rhythm or melody?
  5. Which sounds distinguish the six areas?
  6. Which sounds connect adjacent areas naturally?
  7. Which sounds should remain distinctive to this collection?
  8. Which sounds should be avoided to prevent confusion with other collections?

Potential sources include licensed sound libraries, field recordings, direct recordings, synthesis, and sound-generation tools. External sounds should only be used when the applicable permission and license are clear. If recording or retrieval is unavailable, sounds may be synthesized or processed. The source must not be misreported.

05

Six-Dimension Sound Mapping

Each of the six areas in a collection receives its own sound map. The map may include the following fields:

  1. Official area name
  2. Core concept
  3. Representative environmental sound
  4. Representative symbolic sound
  5. Relevant human, biological, or object sounds
  6. Rhythm-forming sounds
  7. Musical instruments and timbres
  8. Sounds for emotion and spatial character
  9. Key sound effects
  10. A transition sound that may connect to the next area

Sound types, counts, density, duration, and intensity are not fixed across the six areas. One area may require multiple sound layers; another may be expressed with a single subtle sound. Transitions between areas may be used when effective, but are not mandatory.

One work, not six disconnected scenes

The six areas should retain their individual sonic identities while contributing to one coherent musical work. For example, wind may gradually become electronic vibration, water drops may become rhythm, a clock tick may slow into a sustained low tone, or metallic resonance may continue as harmony.

06

Sound Transformation Engine

Time & Speed

Time-stretching, time compression, reverse playback, repetition, rearrangement, speed changes, and rhythmic slicing.

Pitch & Timbre

Pitch shifts, filtering, resonance changes, timbral transformation, harmonic control, distortion, and modulation.

Space

Reverberation, delay, distance, stereo width, near/far contrast, indoor/outdoor characteristics, and spatial movement.

Structure

Sample slicing, repetition, layering, combining effects with instruments, gradual change, and sound emergence or disappearance.

Natural or mechanical sounds may be transformed to function like instruments: water drops as percussion, birdsong as melodic material, wind as a sustained harmonic space, metallic impacts as rhythmic accents, clock ticks as pulse structures, breath as a synthesized waveform, or engine sounds as repeating low-frequency patterns.

07

Human Perception, Sensory Sound & Silence

Sound may suggest space, distance, pressure, movement, tension, release, isolation, expansion, or stillness. The framework proposes using filtering, level, stereo width, reverberation, frequency, and dynamics to create safe perceptual impressions, such as an underwater perspective, a narrowing or expanding space, a sudden quiet, loss of directional impression, or a sound that appears to recede.

Listening-Safety Rules

  • Avoid abrupt, excessive changes in loudness.
  • Avoid potentially harmful extreme high frequencies.
  • Avoid excessive low frequencies or unnecessary physical vibration.
  • Avoid unnecessarily sharp or painful sounds.
  • Avoid repetitive impacts that create listening fatigue.
  • Represent muffled hearing through safe audio processing, not physical stimulation.
  • Check that the result remains comfortable on ordinary stereo headphones and speakers.

Sound Density

SPARSEMODERATEDENSEEXTREME CONTRAST

Density may change during a work. Complex passages can be followed by simple passages, and silence can be used to create tension and release. Silence is treated as part of composition, not as an absence of design.

08

Original Music Composition & Sonic Identity

Each work receives its own compositional principles. The framework asks the creator to determine a core musical motif, rhythm, tempo, meter, melody, harmony, instruments, timbres, sound effects, spatial character, tension, emotional development, transitions among the six areas, introduction, and ending.

Genres may be combined according to the subject, including orchestral music, ambient, electronic music, minimalism, experimental and contemporary music, world music, musique concrète, sound art, non-linear composition, repetition and transformation, atonality, and spatial music.

Distinct Sonic Signature

Each collection should have an identifiable sonic signature of approximately three to five seconds, placed naturally at the beginning or within its core motif. Signatures should be distinguishable across the eighteen works.

09

Audio & Video Production

Differentiate the Eighteen Sonic Worlds

Each collection should have its own sonic signature, environmental and symbolic sounds, primary rhythm, central instruments and timbres, spatial identity, sound transformations, effect-use strategy, and musical structure. A sound may recur in multiple works, but its function and treatment should differ. For example, water in EARTH may represent ecological cycles, while water in OCEAN may represent depth, currents, pressure, or marine life.

Audio Quality & Final Output

  • Use suitable high-quality virtual instruments, professional samples, recordings, and appropriately licensed sound effects.
  • Balance instruments and effects; manage frequency conflicts, low end, stereo space, and dynamics.
  • Mix and master; check clipping, distortion, and playback.
  • Final music deliverable: a single MP3 file, 320 kbps, stereo.
  • WAV is not included in the specified final delivery package.
  • Video audio is embedded as AAC.

Final Quality Control & Execution Protocol

Music Review

  • Is the selected subject central to the music?
  • Can the subject be sensed from the music alone?
  • Are all six areas represented and distinguishable?
  • Do the six areas form one coherent work?
  • Do effects support structure and meaning?
  • Are sound density and silence appropriately managed?
  • Is the sonic identity distinct from other collections?
  • Are mixing, mastering, distortion, clipping, and ending checked?

Blind Subject Identification

Where possible, test whether listeners can identify the subject without seeing its title or video. Distinguish internal assessment from actual external-listener testing; do not report an external test unless it was conducted.

Video and File Review

Check that only the two supplied images are used, the transition occurs once and lasts three seconds, images are not distorted or improperly cropped, audio and video durations match, output specifications are correct, files play correctly, filenames are accurate, and publication materials and reports are present.

Automatic Execution Order

The source workflow proceeds from collection-number confirmation and official data selection through image analysis, concept analysis, sound exploration, sound selection, sound mapping, transformation, motif and signature design, composition, mixing and mastering, MP3 export, video assembly, YouTube metadata, reports, file naming, quality checks, and delivery.
